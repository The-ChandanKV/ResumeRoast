# Review guidelines

## Map the job to the resume

- Keep mandatory qualifications, preferred qualifications, and responsibilities distinct. Preserve alternatives such as “Power BI or Tableau” as one requirement. Do not invent a requirement from the job title.
- Quote or name the section, project, or bullet that supports a match. Separate a standalone skill claim from demonstrated use. Treat equivalent terminology as evidence if the underlying work supports it.
- Report gaps as “not evidenced,” “partially evidenced,” or “explicitly unmet.” For partial coverage, state the demonstrated and missing parts. Reserve “explicitly unmet” for a direct conflict in the supplied information.
- Surface only stated eligibility conditions. Coursework and projects count when the posting allows them. Avoid inferring work authorization, protected characteristics, years of experience from academic dates, or private reasons for resume gaps.

## Diagnose and rewrite bullets

- Flag vague ownership, missing methods, unsupported claims, unreadable phrasing, misplaced detail, and missing outcomes where they reduce the bullet's value. A clear, relevant deliverable can be strong without a numerical result.
- Preserve the individual's actual contribution, tools, project scope, measured results, and the distinction between individual and team work. Prefer an action, object or method, and a verified outcome or purpose when supported.
- Repeat the original wording exactly, apart from omitting unnecessary personal contact details. Explain the weakness and its effect on the reader. Explain what the rewritten version clarifies.
- If the resume only says “Used a database,” but notes document PostgreSQL queries for filtering by category and date, write “Wrote PostgreSQL queries to filter lost-item listings by category and date.” If impact was never measured, leave it out or give a conditional placeholder for a metric the candidate can verify.

## Improve keywords and format

- Recommend the posting's terms when supported and give a location for each. Example: put “SQL” next to the PostgreSQL project work when the notes establish SQL use. List unsupported terms as gaps or learning targets with conditional guidance for future use.
- Inspect headings, section order, date consistency, scannability, bullet length, unexplained acronyms, and duplicated or buried evidence. Assess page layout, margins, columns, and font only when visible. Never assert that a specific ATS will reject the file.
- Prioritize fixes to misleading or missing evidence for mandatory qualifications, then visibility of strong relevant evidence, then presentation polish. Keep “critical” for issues that materially weaken the application. If fewer than five distinct actions are warranted, provide those actions and explain the reduced list.
