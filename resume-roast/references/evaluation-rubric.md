# Resume evaluation rubric

Use whole points. Each category rewards a different property of the submitted resume. Record a short evidence-based justification for each score, then add the five integers for the score out of 100. The total is a custom resume assessment score. It does not reproduce an employer's ATS score or predict hiring.

| Category | Maximum | Scoring method |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Technical skills alignment | 30 | Identify distinct required technical skills, grouping equivalent terms and alternatives. Score each as 1 for direct evidence of applied use, 0.5 for a listed skill or partial evidence, and 0 for no support. Multiply average coverage by 24. Repeat for preferred technical skills with 6 points. If only one tier exists, allocate all 30 to it. Round the combined result once. Exclude academic and other eligibility requirements here. |
| Experience and project relevance | 25 | Allocate up to 10 for relevance to core responsibilities, 8 for clear ownership and scope, and 7 for depth of execution or validation. For each component, use the component anchors below. Credit coursework, projects, and transferable examples on observed merit. |
| Keyword alignment | 20 | Select up to ten distinct, high-signal posting terms before scoring, prioritizing required skills, responsibilities, and explicit qualifications. Use 1 for a supported term visible in a conventional resume section, 0.5 for supported wording that's indirect or buried in ancillary notes, and 0 for absent or unsupported wording. Multiply average by 20 and round. Count synonyms or alternatives once. Name the terms driving the score. |
| Resume structure and readability | 15 | Give up to 5 each for section organization, clear and consistent formatting, and information hierarchy or scannability. Use the component anchors below. Judge only observable features and disclose limitations in layout visibility. |
| Quantifiable achievements | 10 | Give up to 5 for credible numbers describing relevant scope, workload, coverage, or scale, and 5 for credible numbers describing outcomes or evaluation results. Use the component anchors below. Dates alone do not count. Placeholders earn zero evidence credit. |

For component anchors, convert evidence strength to whole points for that component, rounding halves up. Use 0% for absent evidence, 25% for limited evidence, 50% for moderate evidence, 75% for substantial evidence with a material gap, and 100% for clear, sufficient evidence for the role and career stage. Round halves up when calculating any category. When a posting gives no technical requirements or preferences, score that category from evidence of the capabilities needed for its stated responsibilities and describe the adaptation. If the posting has no meaningful requirements or responsibilities, ask for the full posting before scoring.

For the technical and keyword categories, show the coverage total over the number of distinct items and the weight for each applicable tier. Keep full precision until the final category rounding. Classify a bundled requirement consistently across the report. For example, if the report treats the ability to explain data structures as partial, count it as 0.5 when it is scored as a single technical item. If you score the underlying use and the explanation as different items, name both and show each coverage value.

### Worked scoring example

If four required technical skills have coverage values of 1, 1, 0.5, and 0, the required portion earns 15/24. If two preferred skills have values of 1 and 0, the preferred portion earns 3/6. Report 18/30 and identify which items were direct, partial, and absent. With other category scores of 19, 12, 11, and 5, the total is 65/100.
